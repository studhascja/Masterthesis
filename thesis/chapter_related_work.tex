\chapter{Related Work} \label{sec:related}

There are numerous studies investigating the real-time capabilities of Wi-Fi.  
These studies can be divided by their focus.
They prioritize either designing Wi-Fi for real-time applications or improving the real-time capability of Wi-Fi.

Works which focus on Wi-Fi and hard real-time operations, like Wei et al. \cite{6728869} and Moraes et al. \cite{4638758} rely mostly on the \ac{TDMA} approach.  
In both studies, it is reported that hard real-time can only be achieved through a deterministic \ac{MAC}, which \ac{TDMA} was developed for.  
As a result, \cite{6728869} demonstrates that a frequency of real-time packets of up to 6 kHz can be sustained.  

Avdotin et al. \cite{Avdotin_2019} also address the \ac{MAC} layer, but specifically for \ac{OFDMA}.  
In this study, resource allocation within \ac{OFDMA} is adapted, enabling hard real-time capability with cycle times as low as 1 ms.  
This contradicts the findings of \cite{6728869} and \cite{4638758}, which suggest that only \ac{TDMA} can guarantee hard real-time.  
Nevertheless, firmware modifications are still required in this approach.  

There are also studies examining the impact of \ac{QoS} parameters on real-time performance.  
For example, Tsolakou et al. \cite{Tsolakou_2002s} analyze the influence of different \ac{Qdisc} disciplines on real-time capability.  
However, their results are obtained purely through simulations.  
Similarly, Dahoud et al. \cite{da_2019} study the effects of various \ac{DSCP} values on latency, considering transmission time, packet loss and jitter.  
They conclude that the \ac{DSCP} values CS4 and EF result in the lowest latencies.  

Beyond real-time aspects, several works examine the general performance of Wi-Fi standards.  
Chotalia et al. \cite{10.1007/978-3-031-40564-8_14}, for instance, describe performance metrics across standards 4–6, with a particular focus on achievable throughput.  

This raises the question of how the findings of these works relate to the goal of this thesis.  
As described in Chapter \ref{subsec:objective}, the goal of this thesis is to determine the minimum cycle time for real-time data without fundamentally altering the Wi-Fi standard.  
Consequently, approaches such as \ac{TDMA} or modifications of \ac{OFDMA} resource allocation are excluded, as they require changes to the firmware.  
Furthermore, many studies, such as \cite{10.1007/978-3-031-40564-8_14}, are based solely on simulations or controlled laboratory setups.  
In contrast, this thesis also considers non-laboratory environments and their impact on data transmission.  
Additionally, the focus is not only on transmitting real-time data but also on maximizing concurrent best-effort traffic.  
The combination of operating in both laboratory and non-laboratory environments, avoiding modifications to the standard, maximizing concurrent data traffic and testing across multiple standards and \ac{QoS} configurations has not been addressed in prior research.
