\chapter{Conclusion and outlook}\label{sec:conc}
In this chapter, the degree of real-time capability of Wi-Fi is discussed.  
The focus lies primarily on summarizing the results presented in Chapter~\ref{sec:eval} and correlating them with the research question of this thesis.  
Subsequently, open research suggestions derived from the findings of this work are presented.  
Furthermore, an outlook on the future development of Wi-Fi's real-time capability is provided.  

\section{Conclusion}
The research question formulated in Chapter~\ref{subsec:problem} was:
\textit{"What options exist to improve the real-time capability of Wi-Fi, which factors influence it and to what degree can real-time capability be achieved?"}.  
To address this research question, it was divided into three sub-questions.  

The first sub-question, \textit{"What options exist to improve the real-time capability?"}, was examined in Chapters~\ref{sec:implementation} and~\ref{sec:eval}.  
Three main approaches to enhance real-time capability were identified:  
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Wi-Fi configuration:}  
    Optimization can be achieved by adjusting the factors standard, frequency and bandwidth.  
    The best results were obtained using the 6\,GHz band with a bandwidth of 160\,MHz, which corresponds to the Wi-Fi~6 standard.  

    \item \textbf{\ac{QoS}:}  
    Real-time capability can be significantly improved by implementing \ac{QoS}.  
    In this work, \ac{DSCP} values and a \ac{Qdisc} implementation were utilized.  
    The \ac{DSCP} class \ac{EF} combined with a prioritized \ac{HTB} as \ac{Qdisc} achieved the best results.  

    \item \textbf{Environment:}  
    The test environment also plays a decisive role.  
    Wi-Fi's real-time capability can be improved by up to a factor of 40 in interference-free or low-interference environments, although such conditions are typically limited in practice.  
\end{enumerate}

The second sub-question, \textit{"Which factors influence the real-time capability of Wi-Fi?"}, was partly addressed in the discussion of the first.  
In this study, the influencing factors Wi-Fi standard, frequency, bandwidth, \ac{QoS}, and protocol were identified and analyzed.  
The degree to which each factor affects capability varies across test runs and is presented in Table~\ref{tab:eval}.  
The results presented as radar charts in Figures~\ref{fig:r_mid_max}, \ref{fig:r_labor_max} and~\ref{fig:r_high_max} are illustrated as influences in percentage form.  
In other words, the magnitude of each influencing factor is represented as a percentage and the sum of all five factors equals 100\%.

For the evaluation of the strength of the influencing factors, only the impact on the maximum jitter is considered, since the average jitter is not relevant for hard real-time capability.  
It becomes evident that \ac{QoS} represents the most significant influencing factor, while the Wi-Fi standard has the least impact.  
Furthermore, it is notable that the frequency exerts a particularly strong influence under medium interference conditions.  

As a sixth influencing factor, the \textbf{environment} can be taken into account.  
The test run conducted without interference exhibits, on average, only half the maximum jitter compared to the run with medium interference.  
In the high-interference scenario, the maximum jitter is, on average, twenty times higher than under low-interference conditions.  
Although this factor cannot be easily controlled, or in most cases, cannot be influenced at all, it has a stronger impact on real-time capability than any of the other factors discussed and should therefore be also considered as an influencing factor. 

The third sub-question, \textit{"To what degree can real-time capability be achieved in Wi-Fi?"}, is challenging to answer conclusively.  
As described in Section~\ref{subsec:disc}, a 99.999\% deadline compliance probability can already be achieved for a deadline of 3.7\,ms.  
However, the corresponding \ac{WCET} exceeds 700\,ms for this test run.  
Therefore, it can be concluded that Wi-Fi may be suitable for \textbf{soft real-time} and potentially for \textbf{firm real-time} systems, but for \textbf{hard real-time} applications, the deadlines would need to be set significantly higher.  
It must also be ensured that interference is minimized, as it can increase the \ac{WCET} by up to a factor of 24, as indicated by the results in Table~\ref{tab:wcet}.  

The following section discusses the future development of Wi-Fi's real-time capability and explores possible methods to further enhance it.  

\section{Outlook}
Future research building upon this work could extend the experiments conducted here or implement additional methods to further improve real-time capability.  
To extend the experiments of this work, the presented test runs could be repeated in larger network environments instead of a simple peer-to-peer connection, introducing more interference and higher server load.  
Additionally, test runs could be carried out in different physical environments to analyze the effect of surrounding objects on real-time capability, while also varying the communication distance between nodes.  

To improve the real-time capbility, one possible approach is, to use Wi-Fi cards that support multiple driver queues.  
This would allow the prioritization mechanism, implemented in this work via the \ac{Qdisc}, to be extended.  
All Wi-Fi cards used in this study support only a single driver queue, which is why this approach could not be investigated.  

Based on this idea, a scheduler could be developed that dynamically distributes real-time and non-real-time packets according to specific application requirements.  
Such a mechanism could enhance real-time capability for particular use cases without modifying the Wi-Fi standard itself, although it would still constitute a significant modification of the network stack.  

Another possibility to enhance the real-time capability of Wi-Fi is the use of \ac{TDMA} or similar methods to make the \ac{MAC} layer deterministic.  
Such approaches have already been studied and evaluated, as demonstrated by Robson et~al.~\cite{8725178} and Avdotin et~al.~\cite{Avdotin_2019}.  
However, these studies neglected the other influencing factors identified in this work and did not consider different environmental conditions.  
Therefore, a valuable extension of this study would be to repeat all measurements using a deterministic \ac{MAC} and to correlate those results with the findings presented here.  
This would, however, involve a modification of the Wi-Fi standard, which was intentionally avoided in this work and considered undesirable, as discussed in Chapter~\ref{sec:related}.  

In the future, adapting the standard may no longer be necessary.  
As Bartolin-Arnau et~al.~\cite[p.~15]{telecom6020040} note, the real-time capability of Wi-Fi tends to increase with each new standard generation.  
This observation has also been confirmed by the results of this study\footnote{Even though, as described in Chapter~\ref{subsec:disc}, the real-time capability was lowest for the Wi-Fi~6 standard (newest used standard), newer standards inherently introduce improvements in bandwidth and frequency range.
When these accompanying enhancements are taken into account, the statement that real-time capability increases with newer Wi-Fi standards remains valid.}.  
With each new Wi-Fi standard, wider channel bandwidths and sometimes even additional frequency bands are introduced.
Both of these have a positiv impact on the real-time capability.  
Therefore, it can be expected that Wi-Fi~7 will continue this trend and further improve real-time capability.  

Liu et~al.~\cite[p.~3--5]{liu2025ieee80211bewifi7} describe that Wi-Fi~7, also known as IEEE~802.11be, can achieve better real-time capabilities than Wi-Fi~6 due to the introduction of the \ac{MLO} technology, as explained in Chapter~\ref{sec:basics}.  
Moreover, Wi-Fi~7 supports channel bandwidths of up to 320\,MHz, which reduces channel utilization and thus further enhances real-time capability.  

At the time of this study, Wi-Fi~7 has already been released but is not yet widely supported by drivers, such as the \texttt{mt7925e}.  
Consequently, a promising extension of this research would be to replicate the experiments presented in this thesis using Wi-Fi~7, in order to evaluate the individual configuration factors for the new standard.  

Currently, Wi-Fi~8, also referred to as IEEE~802.11bn, is under development.  
Charfi et~al.~\cite[p.~1]{CHARFI2025111590} describe that Wi-Fi~8 aims to improve the \ac{MLO} mechanism and introduce machine learning techniques for optimized scheduling.  
These advancements are expected to further enhance the real-time capability of Wi-Fi.  

In summary, it can be concluded that, as of the time of this work, Wi-Fi without modifications to the standard is not suitable for hard real-time applications.  
Nevertheless, the real-time capability of Wi-Fi continues to improve with each new generation of the standard.  
It is therefore plausible that in the future, even unmodified Wi-Fi systems may be capable of meeting hard real-time requirements.
