\chapter*{Abstract}
In this work, the suitability of Wi-Fi for real-time applications, particularly for systems with hard real-time requirements, is investigated.  
The study considers a time threshold \( x \, \text{ms} \) within which messages can be delivered with a probability of 99.999\%.  
In addition, the WCET of the Wi-Fi connection is examined in order to assess its capability to meet hard real-time constraints.  
Furthermore, the Wi-Fi connection is not intended solely for real-time communication but is also used for the transmission of non-real-time data.

The factors Wi-Fi standard, frequency, bandwidth, QoS and communication protocol are considered in this work to improve the real-time performance of Wi-Fi.  
All tests are conducted on a Raspberry Pi 5 equipped with a MediaTek MT7925B22M Wi-Fi card.  
Wi-Fi standards from Wi-Fi 4 to Wi-Fi 6E are evaluated using a full factorial design.  
The experiments are carried out in three different environments with varying interference levels and an additional long-term test is performed.  
In total, more than 150 different test configurations are examined across the three environments.

The results show that the QoS configuration has the greatest influence on the real-time performance of Wi-Fi.  
The best performance is achieved using DSCP EF in combination with HTB as the queuing discipline.  
It is also observed that configurations using the Wi-Fi 6 standard provide the best real-time capabilities.  
This is mainly attributed to the utilization of the 6 GHz frequency band and the 160 MHz channel bandwidth.  
Finally, it is shown that for a deadline of 3.7 ms, messages can be delivered on time with a probability of 99.999\%.  
However, the WCET of the connection in this case exceeds 700 ms, which makes Wi-Fi only marginally suitable for hard real-time applications.
