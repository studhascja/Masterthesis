\section{Introduction}

``The digital revolution is far more significant than the invention of writing or even of printing.'' stated Douglas Engelbart once \cite{forbes}: 
He thereby classified the invention of the Internet, similarly to Floridi \cite{Floridi12052009}, as the fourth scientific revolution.  
Accordingly, the invention of the Internet and the idea of the ``Galactic Network,'' described by Licklider in 1963, are placed on the same level as the Copernican revolution of 1543 \cite{Kuhn_1959}, in which the Earth was no longer regarded as the center of the universe.  
It is also equated with Darwin's revolution of 1859 \cite{bhl122307}, in which he explained evolution by natural selection rather than divine creation.  
Furthermore, it is compared with Einstein's theory of relativity, which he introduced in 1905 \cite{https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.19053221004}.  

In 1969, the ARPANET was launched \cite[p. 77]{arpanet}, representing the predecessor of the modern Internet and functioning as a wide-area packet-switching network.  
The first intended transmission was the word \textit{LOGIN}, but the connection failed after the first two letters.  
Thus, the very first transmitted packet in history was simply \textit{LO}.  
The Internet in its modern form, namely the World Wide Web, was developed in 1989 by Berners-Lee \cite{www}.  

A significant milestone in the dissemination of the Internet was the development of WaveLAN in 1992 by NCR \cite{6771439}.  
This technology served as the predecessor of \ac{WLAN}, which was standardized in 1997 through IEEE 802.11 \cite{IEEE}.  
As described in \cite{statista}, only 25 years after the introduction of the 802.11 standard, there were already more than half a billion public Wi-Fi hotspots worldwide.  
By 2024, less than 30 years after the introduction of this technology, there are over 5.5 billion users \cite{statista_all}.  

The Internet, however, has not only shaped the private and public spheres of human life.  
It has also found application in industry.  
As Kagermann describes in \cite{kagermann2013industrie4}, the diffusion of the Internet has enabled the emergence of Industry 4.0, following the steam engine, assembly line, and programmable logic controllers, through the use of cyber-physical systems.  
Industry 4.0 already reached a market size of more than 175 billion USD in 2023 and is projected to grow by 20\% annually, reaching more than 625 billion USD by 2030, like described in \cite{industry4}.  
This highlights the decisive role of digitalization in industry, which will only continue to strengthen in the future.  

As Pozzebon emphasizes in \cite{telecom6020040}, \ac{WLAN} plays a crucial role in Industry 4.0.  
It represents a key technology for communication between distributed systems.  
Nevertheless, as outlined in \cite{statista_hem}, there are still obstacles that slow down the dissemination and further development of Industry 4.0.  
These include the susceptibility of systems to interference, high investment costs and the absence of uniform standards.  
Some of these challenges could be mitigated if the key components for communication between devices fulfilled specific requirements and norms.
