\chapter{Introduction}
``The digital revolution is far more significant than the invention of writing or even of printing.'' stated Douglas Engelbart once \cite{forbes}: 
He thereby classified the invention of the Internet, similarly to Floridi \cite{Floridi12052009}, as the fourth scientific revolution.  

A significant milestone in the dissemination of the Internet was the development of WaveLAN in 1992 by NCR \cite{6771439}.  
This technology served as the predecessor of \ac{WLAN}, which was standardized in 1997 through IEEE 802.11 \cite{IEEE}.  
As described in \cite{statista}, only 25 years after the introduction of the 802.11 standard, there were already more than half a billion public Wi-Fi hotspots worldwide.  
By 2024, less than 30 years after the introduction of this technology, there are over 5.5 billion users \cite{statista_all}.  

The Internet, however, has not only shaped the private and public spheres of human life.  
It has also found application in industry.  
As Kagermann describes in \cite{kagermann2013industrie4}, the diffusion of the Internet has enabled the emergence of Industry 4.0, following the steam engine, assembly line, and programmable logic controllers, through the use of cyber-physical systems.  
Industry 4.0 already reached a market size of more than 175 billion USD in 2023 and is projected to grow by 20\% annually, reaching more than 625 billion USD by 2030, like described in \cite{industry4}.  
This highlights the decisive role of digitalization in industry, which will only continue to strengthen in the future.  

As Pozzebon emphasizes in \cite{telecom6020040}, \ac{WLAN} plays a crucial role in Industry 4.0.  
It represents a key technology for communication between distributed systems.  
Nevertheless, as outlined in \cite{statista_hem}, there are still obstacles that slow down the dissemination and further development of Industry 4.0.  
These include the susceptibility of systems to interference, high investment costs and the absence of uniform standards.  
Some of these challenges could be mitigated if the key components for communication between devices fulfilled specific requirements and norms.  

In the following, the problem addressed in this work, is examinated.

\section{Problem description} \label{subsec:problem}
For the previously discussed key component of wireless inter-device communication, a wide variety of standards are currently used, as described in \cite{industry4_standards}.  
These include, as Artexe et al. describe in \cite{industry4_standards}, in addition to Wi-Fi, Bluetooth Low Energy, 5G and five other standards that are most commonly employed.  
This diversity is necessary because no single standard fully satisfies all requirements.  
From this abundance of standards, it becomes clear that, as mentioned, the absence of standardization is often considered problematic.  

Furthermore, real-time capability plays an important role in Industry 4.0, as outlined in \cite[p. 1]{fi16090345}.  
It is also considered that no wireless protocol may be used for tasks requiring real-time capability, as mentioned in \cite{wollert}, instead LAN may be the better choice.  
Nevertheless, there are approaches that fulfill these criteria.  
For example, \cite{fi16090345} presents 5G and Wi-Fi 7 and discusses their suitability for applications in Industry 4.0.  
In this context, the requirement for real-time capability is also taken into account.  

The following section outlines the methodology used in this work and delimits its scope.  

\section{Objective} \label{subsec:objective}
The present work addresses the problem that different standards are used for various application areas.  
Since \ac{WLAN} is one of the most widely deployed wireless protocols in the world and as previously mentioned, is already established in Industry 4.0, this standard is chosen for this study.  
Moreover, access to \ac{WLAN} in industrial environments is easy and cost-effective, as most companies already use \ac{WLAN}.  
As shown in \cite{statista-wifi-indsutry}, in 2023 it accounted for nearly half of Wi-Fi traffic and the proportion of corporate Wi-Fi usage continues to grow.  

One requirement that Wi-Fi does not inherently possess, but which is partly necessary in Industry 4.0, is hard real-time capability.  

The present work therefore addresses the problem in which degree \ac{WLAN} technology is suitable for real-time applications.  
In particular, it investigates to what extent Wi-Fi meets the necessary requirements for reliable communication in industrial environments.  

Thus, this work focuses on the research question:  
What options exist to improve the real-time performance of Wi-Fi, which factors influence it and to what degree can real-time capability be achieved?

\section{Methodology} \label{subsec:meth}
In this work, a test system is developed that can be used to verify that Wi-Fi guarantees that messages can be delivered in x ms in y\% of the cases.  
With this system, it is also evaluated which effects certain adjustments and configurations have on the real-time capability of Wi-Fi.  
Furthermore, this work evaluates the degree of real-time capability that can be achieved with Wi-Fi.  
In other words, it examines which temporal requirements can be fulfilled in a real-time manner using Wi-Fi.  

To investigate this, the \ac{RTT} between server and client is measured.  
This method is illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:method}.
In addition to real-time data, regular data must also be transmitted, as shown in Figure \ref{fig:method}.  
Thus, this work analyzes not only the real-time capability of Wi-Fi but also its ability to handle concurrent best-effort traffic.

This work considers only a single connection between server and client and therefore does not analyze complete \ac{WLAN} networks.  
Moreover, no modifications to the protocol itself are performed.  
On the one hand, such changes would deviate from the standard and potentially create yet another variant, thereby exacerbating the problem described in \cite{statista_hem}.  
On the other hand, \cite{statista_hem} also shows that the greatest obstacle lies in the high investment costs.  
Modifications of the IEEE 802.11 standard would further complicate usage, maintenance and compatibility.  
To avoid these issues, only changes that are comparatively simple to implement are considered, as they introduce no significant development overhead while preserving compatibility with the standard.  

As influencing factors on the real-time capability of Wi-Fi, the standards 4 through 6e are considered.  
Within these, technologies such as \ac{MIMO}, MU-\ac{MIMO}, \ac{OFDMA} and beamforming are investigated.  
In addition, different frequencies and bandwidths are tested regarding their impact on real-time capability.  

Besides standard-dependent technologies, configuration- and implementation-related factors are also taken into account.  
These include network prioritization via \ac{QoS} and \ac{Qdisc}, as well as the influence of different transport protocols such as \ac{UDP} and \ac{TCP}.  

Furthermore, the impact of immutable factors, such as firmware at the MAC layer, on real-time capability is evaluated.  

From these various factors, multiple test cases are derived.  
These are evaluated using the test system in different environments.  
The environments differ primarily in the density of interference sources.  
For particularly promising test cases, long-term measurements are conducted to enable more reliable conclusions about their real-time performance.  

The following section outlines how this objective is structured in this thesis.  

\section{Layout of the work}
This thesis begins with a description of the fundamentals in Chapter \ref{sec:basics}.  
These include, among others, the hardware setup used, the different Wi-Fi standards, and their distinctions.  
Furthermore, the implementation of Wi-Fi in Linux as well as the concept of real-time capability in general and in Linux in particular are addressed in detail.  

After discussing the fundamentals, related literature is presented in Chapter \ref{sec:related} and the existing state of research is analyzed.  
In addition, the scope and objectives of this thesis are distinguished from the existing work.  

Subsequently, the implementation is described in Chapter \ref{sec:implementation}.  
This includes the operating system on which the test system is deployed, as well as the test system itself.  
The test system consists of a test application, an automated test procedure and a monitoring system.  
Each component is explained with respect to its functionality and implementation.  

Afterwards, the execution of the various test cases is described in Chapter \ref{sec:eval}.  
In other words, the implementation of \ac{QoS}, \ac{Qdisc} and the other factors is presented.  
Furthermore, the environment in which the test procedure was conducted is explained.  

The results of the respective test cases are presented in Chapter \ref{sec:eval}.  
Following the description of the test cases and their results, these are discussed in Chapter \ref{sec:eval}.  
The observed behavior is interpreted and conclusions are drawn regarding the influencing factors that have led to these outcomes.  

Finally, Chapter \ref{sec:conc} provides a summary of the results and an outlook.  
This outlook addresses how the real-time capability of \ac{WLAN} might evolve in the future, which additional measures can be investigated to improve the real-time performance of \ac{WLAN} and how \ac{WLAN} could develop in the context of Industry 4.0.
